Why this project exists

I wanted to see what my Mac was doing without paying a subscription or sending telemetry somewhere to find out. The good menu bar monitors are paid, several free ones are abandoned or phone home, and more than one of them costs a noticeable slice of the very thing it is measuring. So I wrote one that does not do any of that.

It is free, MIT licensed, and it will stay that way. There is no paid tier and no feature held back. Everything it measures stays on your machine, and because the source is public you can verify that instead of believing it.

Getting in touch

The most useful place for anything about the app is GitHub, because it keeps the answer visible for whoever asks the same thing next:

  • Bugs and feature requests: open an issue.
  • Questions and general discussion: Discussions.
  • Anything private, including security reports: the Report a vulnerability form on the Security tab. It opens a thread visible only to you and me. See the security policy for what to include.
  • Anything else: LinkedIn.

What I will not do

Worth stating, since trust is the whole point of this page. I do not collect analytics, I do not run a mailing list, I will not add telemetry later, and there is no account to create. The app makes exactly three network requests and the privacy policy names all three.

Honest limitations

This is a spare-time project maintained by one person. That has consequences worth knowing before you rely on it:

  • The app is Apple Silicon only (M1 or later, macOS 14 or newer). It will not run on Intel Macs.
  • It is ad-hoc signed rather than notarised, because an Apple Developer ID costs €99 a year and that is hard to justify for something given away. In practice the first launch needs a right-click, then Open. Updates are verified against an Ed25519 signature regardless.
  • Replies may take days. Bugs get fixed when I have an evening, not within an SLA.
  • The non-English translations were machine-generated and reviewed only for placeholders and layout, not by native speakers. Corrections are genuinely welcome.
